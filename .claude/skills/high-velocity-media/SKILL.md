## 1. Client Proposals

When drafting a proposal, always include:

1. **One-line hook** — open with the client's world, not ours ("Your brand belongs on the mountain. Most crews can't get there.")
2. **Package recommendation** with rationale tied to their specific goal
3. **What's included** — deliverables, formats, number of shoot days, revision rounds
4. **Timeline** — pre-production, shoot days, edit delivery
5. **Investment** — lead with value, present the number confidently
6. **Next step** — one clear action (sign, schedule a call, approve concept)

Keep proposals to one page or two max. The founder's background is the proof — mention it once, powerfully, then let the work speak.

**Tone:** Confident, direct, cinematic. Not sales-y. Think: the brief before a race run — clear, precise, no wasted words.

## 3. Cold Outreach — Brand & Athlete

**Structure for brand outreach (e.g., Salomon, Arc'teryx, local gear makers):**
1. Subject line: specific to their current campaign or season — show you watched
2. Opening: one sentence that proves you know their brand
3. Differentiator: BC D1 background, on-mountain capability — two sentences max
4. Ask: 20-minute call, not a project commitment
5. Social proof or reel link

**Structure for athlete / program outreach (e.g., prep schools, college programs):**
1. Lead with their athlete's goal (recruitment video, brand sponsorship, highlight reel)
2. Position the reel as a tool that works for them 24/7
3. Reference BC Athletics connection if applicable
4. Clear deliverable, timeline, and price

**Tone:** Peer-to-peer. Not vendor-to-buyer. The founder is an athlete reaching out to athletes and people who work with athletes — that credibility opens doors a generic videographer never could.

## 4. Shoot Planning & Equipment Checklist

When planning a mountain or outdoor shoot, always think in four layers:

**Layer 1 — Capture**
- Primary: RED or Sony FX series (high frame rates for slow-motion action)
- Drone: DJI Mavic Pro (wide establishing) + DJI Avata FPV (tracking, chase)
- Backup body: always bring one
- Memory: 2x the cards you think you need

**Layer 2 — Stabilization**
- Gimbal rig rated for the temperature (lithium batteries die fast in cold)
- Confirm gimbal is calibrated with the actual lens you're shooting

**Layer 3 — Safety & Logistics**
- Weather window: book 3-day buffer around ideal conditions
- Permits: ski resort, National Forest, or private land — confirm 2 weeks out
- Athlete briefing: communicate camera positions before every run
- Spotter if shooting at speed

**Layer 4 — Post**
- Log footage same day to two drives (never one)
- Create a rough selects folder within 24 hrs while energy is fresh
- Color grade in DaVinci Resolve; timeline management in Premiere Pro

**Cold-weather tips (racer knowledge):**
- Keep drone batteries inside jacket until launch
- Lens fogging on the way inside from cold — give camera 20 min to acclimate
- Shoot in the golden hour windows even when light is low — the compression from low sun on snow is cinematic gold

## 5. Social Media & Content Strategy

**For the Slalom package / social-first clients:**

Content pyramid:
- **Anchor content (1/month):** 60–90 sec cinematic piece — the one clients share
- **Proof content (2–3/week):** BTS clips, behind-the-lens moments, process reels
- **Engagement content (daily):** Stories, polls, raw moments — humanize the brand

Platforms to prioritize (action sports context):
1. **Instagram Reels** — primary portfolio surface
2. **TikTok** — discovery engine; raw/authentic performs well here
3. **YouTube** — home for longer Summit-level cuts; SEO value for "Boston sports videographer"

Always deliver in these formats: 9:16 (vertical), 1:1 (square), 16:9 (horizontal) — one shoot, all platforms.

## 6. Go-To-Market Phases

**Phase 1 (Months 1–3): Proof of Capability**
- Self-fund a spec ski commercial — one hero shot, one story arc, three social cuts
- This becomes the reel that opens every pitch meeting
- Host on a clean portfolio site (GitHub Pages or Squarespace)
- Target keywords: "Boston sports videographer," "action sports production New England," "outdoor brand commercial Boston"

**Phase 2 (Months 4–6): BC Network Activation**
- Reach out to BC Athletics, club sports, BC alumni in sports marketing and Boston tech
- Alumni rate offer for first 3 clients → 3 case studies → credibility flywheel
- Goal: 1 paid shoot/month minimum by end of Phase 2

**Phase 3 (Months 7–12): Brand & Retainer Push**
- Pitch outdoor gear brands with the Phase 2 reel and case studies
- Target the Slalom retainer model: 2–3 social-first clients at $2,500–$4,000/month = predictable base revenue
- Begin SEO content: blog posts, YouTube tutorials on mountain shooting, BTS vlogs

## 7. Financial Projections Framework

When building projections, model three scenarios:

| Scenario | Monthly Revenue | How |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative | $3,000–$4,000 | 1 Downhill + 1 Slalom project/month |
| Base | $7,000–$10,000 | 2 Downhill + 1 Summit + 1 Slalom retainer |
| Growth | $15,000–$25,000 | 3 Downhill/Summit + 2–3 Slalom retainers |

**Key expenses to model:**
- Equipment depreciation / rental buffer: ~$500/mo
- Software (Premiere Pro, DaVinci, storage, Adobe Suite): ~$200/mo
- Insurance (gear + liability — non-negotiable): ~$150–$300/mo
- Website / portfolio hosting: ~$30/mo
- Travel / fuel for location shoots: variable, pass through to client

**Rule of thumb:** Bill equipment-intensive shoots with a line-item gear fee (10–15% of total production budget). It's transparent, professional, and protects your margins.

## 8. Tone & Voice Guidelines

Every client-facing document — proposals, emails, invoices, social captions — should feel like it came from the same person:

- **Precise** — no filler words; racers don't narrate their lines, they run them
- **Confident, not arrogant** — the BC background is a credential, not a brag
- **Visual** — use active, sensory language: "we'll put the camera where no crew goes"
- **Direct ask** — every communication ends with one next step, never two
