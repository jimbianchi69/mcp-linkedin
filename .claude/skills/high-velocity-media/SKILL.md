# High-Velocity Media — Career Strategy Guide

## The Mission

**Goal:** Land a production role at an elite sports media organization.
**Two Priority Targets:** Boston Celtics (digital/social content) and Religion of Sports (documentary storytelling)
**Unique Asset:** Boston College D1 Ski Racer — a credential that creates two distinct competitive edges depending on the target.
**Status:** Warm introductions exist to both companies. The goal is to make those intros impossible to ignore.

---

## Target Analysis: Know Their Creative DNA

Before building any content, understand exactly what each organization values. Generic reels fail. Tailored spec work wins.

### Boston Celtics — Digital & Social Content

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Vibe** | Fast-paced, high-energy, real-time social integration |
| **Content type** | Access-driven hype packages, mic'd-up moments, franchise storytelling for social |
| **What they hire for** | Speed, retention metrics, starting in media res, fan culture fluency |
| **What kills a reel** | Slow fades, title cards, generic music tracks, anything that could be from any sport |

**Your D1 edge here:** You tracked athletes moving at 60+ MPH on ice. A basketball player driving the lane feels like slow motion. Pitch your physical agility — the ability to get angles other shooters miss, to anticipate movement before it happens, to stay locked in under pressure.

---

### Religion of Sports — Documentary Storytelling

| Dimension | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Vibe** | Emmy-level docuseries, deeply human, cinematic and slow-paced |
| **Content type** | Explores why sports matter — vulnerability, failure, grit, the cost of elite performance |
| **What they hire for** | Emotional depth, structural narrative instinct, interview craft, cinematography with intentionality |
| **What kills a reel** | Surface-level content, happy endings without earned tension, footage without a point of view |

**Your D1 edge here:** You have stood at the top of a slalom gate where a hundredth of a second separates success from failure. You understand the loneliness and internal pressure of elite sports in a way standard journalists never will. That shared psychology lets you interview athletes at a depth that produces raw, watchable vulnerability — which is exactly what Religion of Sports is built on.

## The Two Spec Projects to Build

Do not rely on student work. Build two premium spec pieces, each tailored to one lane.

### Spec Project 1: "The Mic'd Up" — For the Celtics Lane

**Format:** 60-second training package, hyper-stylized, sound-design-heavy
**Subject:** A high-level local athlete — BC player, elite runner, boxer, or hockey player
**Execution:**
- Start in media res: first frame is action, collision, or explosive movement — never a title card or fade
- Build the audio world: heavy breathing, squeaking shoes, ball impact, crowd noise fragments
- Cut to the rhythm of the sound design, not just the music
- Deliver a hook in the first 3 seconds — social teams measure retention from frame one

**Why this wins:** It proves you understand that sports digital content lives or dies by its opening three seconds and its audio environment.

---

### Spec Project 2: "Agony of the Line" — For the Religion of Sports Lane

**Format:** 3-minute mini-documentary
**Subject:** An athlete overcoming a defining hurdle — ACL recovery, a devastating loss, the pressure of a final season, a cut from a team
**Execution:**
- Conduct an interview that gets genuinely vulnerable — your D1 background is what earns that trust
- Structure the arc: setup → tension → transformation (even if incomplete)
- Intercut b-roll that is cinematic and intentional, not decorative
- Use slow pacing — let silence breathe; resist the urge to fill every moment
- Sound design: the creak of a weight room, the sound of a hospital corridor, the quiet of an empty gym

**Why this wins:** It proves you can find and protect the human story underneath the athletic one.

## The Three Portfolio Craft Priorities

### 1. Master Sound Design (Both Targets)

Sports media is 50% audio. A generic hip-hop track or ambient music bed signals that you don't understand the medium.

**What to do:**
- Layer ambient sounds: the slice of a ski edge, the rattle of a rim, the heartbeat before a whistle
- Build diegetic sound into your edit — sounds that exist inside the world of the shot
- Show that you construct an immersive environment, not just footage with a music track

This applies to both spec pieces. Even the fast Celtics cut should have a deliberate audio architecture.

---

### 2. Hook in 3 Seconds (Celtics Lane)

Digital content teams live inside retention metrics. They will not watch a reel that starts slowly.

**The rule:** Never start with a slow fade, a title card, a landscape establishing shot, or a talking head. Start with movement, impact, or tension already in progress.

**Test:** Mute your video. Does the first frame make someone stop scrolling? If not, recut your opening.

---

### 3. Write a Director's Treatment (Both Targets)

Coming from BC rather than a film school, you need to prove structural creative thinking — not just camera skill. A one-page Treatment for each spec project does this.

**What a Treatment includes:**
- The story you're telling and why you chose it
- The athlete and why they're the right subject
- The visual approach — lighting rationale, lens choices, location decisions
- The audio strategy
- The emotional arc the viewer should feel

Attach the Treatment when you send your intro pitch. It signals executive maturity and separates you from every other shooter who just sends a link.

See `references/directors-treatment-template.md` for the full template.

## How to Frame Your BC D1 Background

This is your differentiator. Use this language — or something close to it — in your intro pitch, cover letter, and any follow-up conversation:

> "As a former D1 racer at BC, I spent years analyzing movement, lines, and split-second athletic decisions under pressure. I've transitioned that exact skill set into media production — learning to anticipate the next play before it happens and to capture the raw grit that defines elite performance. I built these two pieces specifically to show how I bring that background to the fast-turnaround energy of [Celtics] / the deep human narratives that define [Religion of Sports]."

**Calibrate by target:**
- **For the Celtics:** Emphasize physical agility, anticipation of movement, and the ability to get angles other crews can't
- **For Religion of Sports:** Emphasize the psychological understanding of elite pressure, shared experience of high-stakes competition, and the ability to build trust with athletes quickly

## Intro Pitch Strategy

Because you have warm introductions — not cold outreach — the approach is different. Your contact is the credibility bridge. Your job is to give the hiring manager or producer a reason to say yes.

**Sequence:**
1. Ask your contact specifically who to reach out to and what context to give
2. Send a short, direct email (not a portfolio dump) — see `references/intro-pitch-templates.md`
3. Include the two spec pieces and the two Treatments
4. Make one clear ask: a 20-minute call or a portfolio review meeting

**What not to do:** Don't send a general reel. Don't send a long email. Don't mention every project you've ever done. Two pieces, each tailored to their lane, is more powerful than ten.

## Gear Reality Check

When planning spec shoots, work with what you have or can rent. The spec projects don't require expensive gear — they require a point of view and craft.

**Minimum viable setup for both projects:**
- A camera that shoots 4K with decent low-light (Sony A7 series, FX30, or a rental)
- A 70–200mm equivalent for compression shots (telephoto makes athletes look powerful)
- A lavalier mic and a shotgun mic (audio is 50% of the product)
- DJI Mavic or Avata for establishing shots if budget allows

**Prioritize:** If you're renting, spend more on audio than on camera body. A great image with weak audio loses immediately. A good image with rich, layered sound wins.
