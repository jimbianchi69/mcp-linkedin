# Director's Treatment Template

A Treatment is a one-page document that proves you think like a storyteller, not just a camera operator. Submit one alongside each spec piece when pitching Religion of Sports or the Celtics. It demonstrates executive maturity and separates you from every other applicant who sends a reel with no context.

---

**Project Title:** [e.g., "Agony of the Line" / "The Mic'd Up"]
**Format:** [60-second training package / 3-minute mini-documentary]
**Subject:** [Name of athlete and brief context — who are they, what are they going through?]
**Target Company Alignment:** [Boston Celtics — digital/social lane / Religion of Sports — documentary lane]

---

## The Story

[2–3 sentences. What is this piece actually about? Not "a basketball player training" — something with tension. "This is about the six months after Marcus tore his ACL in the final game of his senior year — and what it cost him to decide whether to come back." Give the story a spine before you shoot a frame.]

---

## Why This Subject

[Why this athlete, this moment, this story? What makes them the right lens for what you're trying to say? If you chose them because you have access or a personal connection, say so — authenticity of access is a real creative asset.]

---

## Visual Approach

**Lighting:** [e.g., "Available light only — gym fluorescents and exterior winter light. The coldness of the environment mirrors the emotional state."]

**Lenses:** [e.g., "70–200mm for compression on the athlete, making the space feel claustrophobic. 24mm handheld for the interview — intimate and slightly unstable."]

**Camera movement:** [e.g., "Locked shots for the interview. Kinetic, tracking movement for the training sequences."]

**Color direction:** [e.g., "Desaturated, cool tones. Not stylized — honest."]

---

## Audio Strategy

[This section matters as much as the visual section. How are you building the sound world?]

**Ambient/diegetic sound:** [e.g., "The squeak of shoes on the gym floor, the rattle of weights, the breath between sets — all captured live and layered in post."]

**Music:** [e.g., "Minimal. A single piano line that enters only after the emotional turn. No music during the interview."]

**Interview approach:** [e.g., "Conversational, not formal. I'll start by asking about the injury in clinical terms, then pivot to how it felt to watch the team from the bench. That's where the vulnerability surfaces."]

---

## Emotional Arc

[Describe the feeling you want the viewer to have at each stage — not what happens, but how it should feel.]

| Moment | Intended Feeling |
|---|---|
| Opening 10 seconds | [e.g., "Disorientation — something happened here"] |
| Middle | [e.g., "Recognition — this athlete is fighting something real"] |
| Final 30 seconds | [e.g., "Unresolved tension — we don't know if they make it back"] |

---

## Why My Background Makes This Piece Possible

[One short paragraph. This is where the D1 background becomes an asset — not bragging, but explaining why you could access this story in a way another filmmaker couldn't.]

Example: *"I've stood at the top of a D1 slalom course where a hundredth of a second separates a result from a disaster. That shared experience meant Marcus trusted me in the interview in a way he said he hadn't trusted other cameras. You can hear it in the audio — the pauses get longer. That's the moment."*
