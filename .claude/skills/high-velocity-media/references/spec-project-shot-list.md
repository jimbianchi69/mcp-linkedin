# Spec Project Shot Lists

Two spec projects, two distinct visual languages. Read the target analysis in SKILL.md before shooting either of these.

---

## Spec Project 1: "The Mic'd Up"
### Target: Boston Celtics Digital & Social Lane
### Format: 60-second training package

**Pre-Shoot Checklist**

- [ ] Athlete confirmed and briefed (BC player, elite runner, boxer, or hockey player)
- [ ] Location secured: gym, court, track, or ice
- [ ] Lavalier mic fitted to athlete (capture breathing and movement sounds)
- [ ] Shotgun mic on camera for ambient room tone
- [ ] Memory cards: 2x what you expect
- [ ] Camera set to high frame rate for slow-motion options (120fps minimum)
- [ ] Opening shot planned — remember: first frame must hook, no fade-in

**Audio Priority (do not skip)**
- [ ] Record 2 minutes of room tone before athlete arrives
- [ ] Capture isolated sounds: shoe squeak on floor, ball bounce, weight rack rattle, deep breath between sets
- [ ] These get layered in post — they're as important as the picture

---

### Shot List

#### Opening Hook (must be the first 3 seconds of the edit)

| # | Shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Explosive movement — athlete mid-action | No build-up. Start here. |
| 1.2 | Low-angle speed — athlete driving past camera | Camera at knee height; athlete passes within 1m |
| 1.3 | Handheld tight — face during max effort | Get sweat, breath, intensity |

#### Action Sequences (the body of the piece)

| # | Shot | Lens | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Full-speed tracking — athlete across the frame | 70–200mm | Stay with them; don't let them exit frame |
| 2.2 | Telephoto compression — athlete moving toward camera | 200mm | Makes the space feel contained and intense |
| 2.3 | Slow-motion impact moment | High FPS | Ball hitting floor, foot planting, jump peak |
| 2.4 | Over-the-shoulder POV | 24mm | Makes viewer feel inside the movement |
| 2.5 | Repeat each key move 3–4 times | | More options in the edit |

#### Texture / Detail Shots (sound design layer)

| # | Shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Hands on the ball / grip tape / laces | Close — isolate the texture |
| 3.2 | Shoe sole on floor — low angle | Capture the squeak live |
| 3.3 | Breath visible in cold air (if outdoor / cold gym) | |
| 3.4 | Sweat on face, neck, arm | Effort is visible |
| 3.5 | Eyes — pre-set focus, pre-jump | This is the Religion of Sports shot; use it here for contrast |

#### Closing Shot

| # | Shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Athlete stopping, looking up / out | End on stillness after movement — contrast lands the emotion |

---

### Edit Notes for This Piece

- Cut to the sound design, not to the music
- Keep the first cut under 75 seconds, then tighten to 60
- No title card, no end card with your name — let the work end cleanly
- Export in both 16:9 and 9:16 vertical

## Spec Project 2: "Agony of the Line"
### Target: Religion of Sports Documentary Lane
### Format: 3-minute mini-documentary

**Pre-Shoot Checklist**

- [ ] Athlete confirmed and story arc understood — what is the hurdle? (injury, loss, final season, being cut)
- [ ] Interview location scouted — a meaningful place, not a neutral background
- [ ] Lavalier mic fitted; backup recorder running simultaneously
- [ ] Interview questions written (see below)
- [ ] B-roll locations identified: training, home, the place where the defining moment happened
- [ ] Signed release from athlete

**Interview Prep (critical for this project)**

The interview is the spine of this piece. Do not wing it.

Questions to move through — in this order:
1. Start clinical: "Walk me through what happened, factually." (Gets them comfortable)
2. Move to physical: "What did it feel like in your body in that moment?"
3. Go psychological: "What was the hardest part that no one saw?"
4. Find the quiet truth: "Was there a moment where you thought about stopping? What happened in that moment?"
5. Leave space: After their answer, wait 5 seconds before speaking. The real answer often comes in the silence.

Your BC background earns you the right to ask question 4. Use it.

---

### Shot List

#### Interview Setup

| # | Shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| I.1 | Single-camera interview, eye-line slightly off lens | Not directly at camera — more naturalistic |
| I.2 | Tight insert: hands during emotional moments | Cut to hands when voice breaks; gives viewer permission to process |
| I.3 | Wide of location with athlete in frame | Establish where we are and why it matters |

#### B-Roll: Training / Effort Sequences

| # | Shot | Lens | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| B.1 | Athlete in their environment | 70–200mm | Let them move naturally; don't direct |
| B.2 | The specific movement that relates to their injury / challenge | Slow-motion | This is the image you'll return to |
| B.3 | Rest moments — sitting, looking, breathing | Wide or medium | Stillness is content here |
| B.4 | The space when it's empty | Wide | Empty gym, empty track, empty slope |
| B.5 | Details of the sport: equipment, tape, scars if appropriate | Macro / tight | Objects carry memory |

#### Location / Atmosphere

| # | Shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| L.1 | Where the defining moment happened (if accessible) | Could be a court, a mountain, a hospital corridor |
| L.2 | Arriving at the location with athlete | Handheld, following — let the arrival carry weight |
| L.3 | Exterior of training facility | Dawn or dusk light; not midday |

#### Closing Image

| # | Shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| C.1 | Athlete alone in their environment — still, looking | Do not direct this; ask them to stand somewhere meaningful and wait |
| C.2 | Pull back slowly or cut to black | The ending should feel unresolved — that's honest |

---

### Edit Notes for This Piece

- Structure: open with a fragment of the interview that raises a question, then build to the moment that answers it
- Let silence breathe — do not fill every beat with music or narration
- Music enters only after the emotional turn; not before
- Color: desaturated, honest, not stylized
- Length: aim for 3 minutes; 3:30 maximum
- No lower-thirds or titles — let the subject speak without labels
